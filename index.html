Different ways to learn. Equal room to grow.

See it. Try it. Make it yours.

The program puts visual, hands-on learning at the centre. Children explore ideas through demonstrations, picture-based instructions and building together—not through sound-dependent activities.

Visual-first

Show, then do

Picture cards, clear demonstrations, visual agendas and timers make each activity easier to follow.

Hands-on

Ideas you can build

LEGO robotics, icon-based coding and practical challenges give children ways to explore and show what they know.

Together

A role for everyone

Children rotate through building, programming, testing, documenting and presenting, with time for visual communication.

The learning journey

Small discoveries. Real possibilities.

Six connected themes introduce robotics, coding and AI. Activities are adapted to the group, with opportunities to experiment, make mistakes and try again.

01

Meet your robot

Explore sensors, build a robot and use visual code to make it respond to colour.

02

Teach a machine

Sort colourful objects and discover how an image classifier learns from examples.

03

Choose a path

Use if/then logic to help a robot make decisions and navigate a maze.

04

Explore computer vision

Train an image model with objects and try a find-and-deliver robot challenge.

05

Test. Measure. Improve.

Record robot trials, change your code and use visual charts to see what improves.

06

Show what you made

Bring your ideas together in a smart-warehouse challenge and share your creation.

These are curriculum themes, not a published timetable. The 2026 delivery adapted them into week-long camp groups. Robot coding and hands-on testing sit alongside separate image-classification activities; not every robot task uses machine learning.

Good to know

Questions from families

Does my child need coding experience?

The curriculum begins with robot building and simple visual programming, then develops toward more complex challenges. Activities can be simplified or extended for different learners.

How does the program support deaf and hard-of-hearing learners?

The curriculum uses visual instructions, demonstrations, picture cards, icon-based coding and visual timers. It encourages sign-language communication and allows time for visual interaction. Confirm specific language support and interpreter availability with the organiser; the documents do not guarantee these services.

What ages is the program for?

The original curriculum includes adaptations for ages 5–12. The completed 2026 camp served children aged 7–12. The age range for future groups is still to be confirmed.

Is teaching delivered in English?

This webpage is in English. The language or languages of instruction for future sessions have not been confirmed. Families should check teaching language and sign-language arrangements with the organiser.

When and where are the next sessions?

The 2026 program has finished. AurinkoLab ry, based in Espoo, plans to continue the program in 2026/27, but the supplied documents do not confirm a new timetable, venue address, fees or registration process.
